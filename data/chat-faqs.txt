Q: What is a personal loan?
A: A personal loan is usually an unsecured loan taken by an individual for personal needs such as medical expenses, education costs, travel, a wedding or consolidating existing debt. It generally does not require property as collateral, but the lender checks income, credit history and repayment capacity. The interest rate and terms depend on the lender and your profile.
PAGE: personal-loan
TAGS: personal loan meaning, unsecured loan, individual loan, personal expenses

---

Q: What can I use a personal loan for?
A: Depending on the lender's policy, a personal loan may be used for medical expenses, education, travel, home repairs, family needs or other permitted personal purposes. Some lenders restrict certain uses. Always provide the actual purpose in your application and check the lender's terms.
PAGE: personal-loan
TAGS: personal loan purpose, usage, medical, wedding, travel, home repair

---

Q: What is a secured loan?
A: A secured loan is backed by an asset offered as collateral, such as a house, land or vehicle. If the borrower does not repay as agreed, the lender may take recovery action against the secured asset under applicable law and the loan agreement. Review the risks carefully before pledging an asset.
PAGE: loans
TAGS: secured loan, collateral, security, asset

Q: What is an unsecured loan?
A: An unsecured loan does not require the borrower to pledge a specific asset as collateral. Personal loans are commonly unsecured. Since the lender does not have a pledged asset as security, it may place greater weight on income, credit history and repayment capacity.
PAGE: personal-loan
TAGS: unsecured, no collateral, personal loan

Q: What is a business loan?
A: A business loan is financing used for business needs such as working capital, inventory, expansion, equipment or operational expenses. Lenders may assess business vintage, turnover, profitability, bank statements, tax filings, existing obligations and the owner's credit profile.

Q: What is working capital?
A: Working capital is the money a business uses for day-to-day operations, such as buying stock, paying suppliers, wages and routine expenses. A working-capital loan may help manage a temporary cash-flow gap, but the repayment schedule should fit the business's expected cash flow.

TAGS: working capital, cash flow, business expenses, inventory

Q: What is a home loan?
A: A home loan is financing used to purchase, construct or sometimes renovate a residential property, depending on the lender's product. The property is generally mortgaged to the lender until the loan is repaid. The lender checks income, credit history, property documents and legal or technical details.

Q: What is a loan against property?
A: A loan against property (LAP), also called a mortgage loan, allows an eligible property owner to borrow by offering a property as collateral. The lender assesses the property's value, ownership documents, legal status and the applicant's repayment capacity. If repayments are not made, the pledged property may be at risk.

A: A home loan is generally used to buy, construct or improve a home. A loan against property uses an already-owned eligible property as collateral to raise funds for a permitted purpose. Both are secured loans, but the purpose, documentation, loan-to-value limits and lender terms can differ.

Q: What are the basic eligibility criteria for a loan?
A: Lenders commonly consider your age, income, employment or business stability, credit history, existing EMIs, repayment capacity, location and required documents. Secured loans may also involve property or asset checks. Each lender has its own policy, so eligibility is assessed case by case.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: eligibility, qualify, criteria, age, income, employment, business

---

Q: What is a CIBIL score?
A: A CIBIL score is a three-digit number that summarises information in your credit history maintained by TransUnion CIBIL. Lenders may use it, along with income and other details, when assessing a credit application. A higher score is generally viewed more favourably, but no single score guarantees approval.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: cibil, credit score, transunion cibil, credit history

---

Q: What is considered a good CIBIL score?
A: A score around 750 or above is commonly viewed as strong by many lenders, but their cut-offs differ by product and applicant. A lower score does not automatically mean every application will be rejected, and a high score does not guarantee approval. Lenders also assess income, existing debt and repayment history.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: good cibil, cibil range, 750, credit score range

---

Q: Can I get a loan with a low CIBIL score?
A: It may be possible with some lenders or products, but the options may be limited and the terms may be less favourable. Approval depends on the full profile, including income, repayment history, existing obligations and lender policy. Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed approval for a fee.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: low cibil, bad credit, poor score, loan with low score

---

Q: How can I improve my credit score?
A: Pay EMIs and credit-card dues on time, keep credit-card utilisation under control, avoid unnecessary loan applications, review your credit report for errors and maintain a healthy mix of credit over time. Improvements are not instant; the bureau updates information as lenders report it. Sobhakrita cannot guarantee a particular score increase.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: improve cibil, improve credit score, credit repair, score increase

---

Q: What is a credit report?
A: A credit report is a record of your borrowing and repayment history reported by lenders to credit information companies. It may include loan and credit-card accounts, balances, payment history, enquiries and account status. Review it periodically and raise disputes with the relevant credit bureau and lender if you find incorrect information.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: credit report, bureau report, loan history, credit file

---

Q: What is a hard enquiry on my credit report?
A: A hard enquiry is generally recorded when a lender checks your credit report as part of a credit application. Several applications in a short period can affect how lenders view your credit profile and may affect your score. Ask which lender will access your report and why before authorising an application.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: hard enquiry, credit enquiry, cibil enquiry, bureau enquiry

Q: Does checking my own CIBIL score reduce it?
A: Checking your own credit report through an authorised self-access facility is generally treated as a soft enquiry and does not reduce your score. A lender's credit check for a new application is generally a hard enquiry. Use official credit-bureau channels and avoid sharing your login credentials.
PAGE: eligibility
TAGS: self check cibil, soft enquiry, own credit score

Q: What is FOIR?
A: FOIR stands for Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio. It compares your regular fixed repayment obligations, such as existing EMIs, with your income. Lenders use it as one way to assess whether you can manage another EMI. The acceptable ratio differs by lender and applicant profile.

Q: What is EMI?
A: EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the amount a borrower pays to the lender at regular monthly intervals to repay a loan. An EMI generally includes a principal component and an interest component. The amount depends mainly on the loan amount, interest rate and repayment tenure.
PAGE: emi-calculator
TAGS: emi, equated monthly instalment, monthly payment, meaning

---

Q: How is EMI calculated?
A: For a standard reducing-balance loan, EMI is calculated using the loan principal, monthly interest rate and total number of monthly instalments. A longer tenure usually lowers the monthly EMI but can increase the total interest paid. The lender's repayment schedule is the final reference for your loan.
PAGE: emi-calculator
TAGS: emi calculation, formula, how calculated, monthly instalment

---

Q: What is the principal amount?
A: The principal is the amount you borrow from the lender, excluding interest. As you pay your EMIs, part of each payment generally reduces the outstanding principal and the remaining part covers interest and any other amounts due under the loan terms.
PAGE: emi-calculator
TAGS: principal, loan amount, outstanding principal

---

Q: What is interest on a loan?
A: Interest is the cost charged by the lender for allowing you to use the borrowed money. It is usually expressed as a percentage rate. The actual cost depends on the rate, calculation method, outstanding balance, tenure and applicable charges.
PAGE: loans
TAGS: interest, interest meaning, cost of borrowing

---

Q: What is the difference between a fixed and floating interest rate?
A: A fixed rate generally remains unchanged for the agreed fixed-rate period, while a floating rate can change based on the benchmark or reset mechanism stated in the loan agreement. A rate change may affect the EMI, tenure or both. Check the lender's agreement for the exact reset rules.
PAGE: loans
TAGS: fixed rate, floating rate, variable interest

---

Q: What is reducing balance interest?
A: Under a reducing-balance method, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal rather than the original loan amount throughout the tenure. As principal is repaid, the interest portion generally reduces over time. The lender's KFS and agreement should explain the method used.
PAGE: emi-calculator
TAGS: reducing balance, diminishing balance, interest method

Q: What is flat interest?
A: Under a flat-rate method, interest is calculated on the original principal for the agreed period rather than on the reducing outstanding balance. The quoted flat rate can therefore look lower than a reducing-balance rate while producing a different total cost. Compare the APR and total repayment before deciding.
PAGE: emi-calculator
TAGS: flat rate, flat interest, flat vs reducing

# L. GENERAL FINANCIAL TERMS CUSTOMERS MAY ASK ABOUT
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q: What is a loan?
A: A loan is money borrowed from a lender that must be repaid according to agreed terms. The borrower usually repays the principal along with interest and any applicable charges over a specified period. Before accepting a loan, understand the EMI, interest rate, tenure, fees and consequences of missed payments.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: loan meaning, borrowing, what is a loan

---

Q: What is the difference between a loan and a credit card?
A: A term loan usually provides a fixed amount that is repaid over an agreed schedule. A credit card provides a revolving credit limit that can be reused as you repay, subject to the card terms. Interest, fees, minimum payments and repayment rules differ, so compare the total cost and avoid paying only the minimum due for long periods.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: loan vs credit card, credit card, revolving credit

---

Q: What is a lender?
A: A lender is a bank, NBFC or other authorised institution that provides money under a credit agreement. It assesses the application, sets the terms, disburses the loan and collects repayments. In a Sobhakrita-facilitated application, the lender is the institution named in your loan documents.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: lender meaning, bank, nbfc, creditor

---

Q: What is an NBFC?
A: NBFC stands for Non-Banking Financial Company. It is a financial company regulated under the applicable Indian regulatory framework and may provide services such as loans and asset finance. An NBFC is not the same as a bank and may not offer every service that a bank does.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: nbfc meaning, non banking financial company

---

Q: What is collateral?
A: Collateral is an asset offered as security for a loan. It may be property, a vehicle, a deposit or another asset accepted by the lender. If the borrower does not repay as agreed, the lender may enforce its rights over the collateral under the agreement and applicable law.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: collateral meaning, security, pledged asset

---

Q: What is a guarantor?
A: A guarantor is a person who agrees to be responsible for repayment if the borrower does not meet the loan obligations, according to the guarantee terms. Becoming a guarantor can have financial and credit consequences, so read the guarantee document carefully before signing.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: guarantor, co signer, guarantee, loan responsibility

---

Q: What is a co-applicant?
A: A co-applicant applies for a loan together with the main applicant. The lender may consider both applicants' income and credit profiles, and both may have repayment responsibilities under the agreement. The exact legal obligations depend on the loan documents.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: co applicant, joint applicant, joint loan

---

Q: What is a down payment?
A: A down payment is the amount the buyer pays from their own funds towards a purchase, with the remaining eligible amount financed by a lender. It is common in vehicle and property purchases. A larger down payment may reduce the amount borrowed, but the lender sets its own margin and funding rules.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: down payment, margin money, own contribution

---

Q: What is LTV?
A: LTV stands for Loan-to-Value ratio. It compares the loan amount with the value of the asset being financed or pledged. Lenders use it to manage risk, and the permitted ratio depends on the product, asset, valuation and applicable rules.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: ltv, loan to value, property value, margin

---

Q: What is a credit limit?
A: A credit limit is the maximum amount a lender allows a customer to borrow or use under a credit facility, subject to its terms. It is not necessarily the amount currently outstanding or the amount that must be repaid immediately.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: credit limit, sanctioned limit, available limit

---

Q: What is a moratorium?
A: A moratorium is a period during which certain repayments may be deferred under the terms approved by a lender or applicable scheme. It does not automatically mean that interest stops accruing or that the loan is waived. Check the written terms to understand how the deferred amount is treated.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: moratorium, payment holiday, repayment pause

---

Q: What is loan restructuring?
A: Loan restructuring is a change to agreed loan terms, such as tenure, repayment schedule or other conditions, made with the lender's approval. It may help address repayment difficulty, but it can affect total interest, credit reporting and other terms. Ask the lender for a written explanation before agreeing.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: restructuring, reschedule loan, change repayment terms

---

Q: What is a loan write-off?
A: A write-off is an accounting action taken by a lender when it removes a loan or part of it from certain books for accounting purposes. It does not automatically mean the borrower's repayment obligation is cancelled. Ask the lender for the current legal and account status if you see this term in a report.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: write off, loan written off, debt, credit report

---

Q: What is a loan settlement?
A: A settlement generally means the lender agrees to accept an amount different from the full contractual dues to resolve an account, subject to written terms. A settled status may affect future credit assessments. Do not agree to a settlement without understanding the amount, written confirmation and credit-reporting consequences.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: loan settlement, one time settlement, ots, debt settlement

---

Q: What is the difference between settled and closed in a credit report?
A: A closed account generally indicates the loan was repaid according to the lender's closure records. A settled account may indicate that the lender accepted less than the full amount due under an agreed settlement. The status can matter to future lenders; check the report and lender records if you believe it is incorrect.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: settled vs closed, loan closed, credit report status

---

Q: What is a bank statement?
A: A bank statement is a record of transactions in a bank account over a selected period. It shows deposits, withdrawals, transfers, charges and balances. Lenders may review it to understand income, cash flow and existing financial commitments.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: bank statement meaning, account statement, transactions

---

Q: What is NEFT?
A: NEFT stands for National Electronic Funds Transfer. It is an electronic system used to transfer money between participating bank accounts in India. Use your bank's official app, website or branch and verify the beneficiary details before making a transfer.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: neft, national electronic funds transfer, bank transfer

---

Q: What is RTGS?
A: RTGS stands for Real Time Gross Settlement. It is an electronic funds-transfer system generally used for high-value transfers, with each transaction settled individually in real time during the system's operating availability. Check your bank's current limits, charges and instructions before transferring funds.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: rtgs, real time gross settlement, high value transfer

---

Q: What is IMPS?
A: IMPS stands for Immediate Payment Service. It enables near-real-time interbank transfers through participating banks, subject to bank limits and availability. Always verify the beneficiary details and use your bank's official channel.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: imps, immediate payment service, instant transfer

---

Q: What is UPI?
A: UPI stands for Unified Payments Interface. It allows users to make digital payments and transfers through supported banking apps using a UPI ID, mobile number or other supported method. Never share your UPI PIN; you generally enter it only to authorise a payment you are making.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: upi, unified payments interface, digital payment

---

Q: What is the difference between NEFT, RTGS, IMPS and UPI?
A: They are different electronic payment systems. NEFT processes bank transfers in batches at frequent intervals, RTGS settles eligible transfers individually in real time, IMPS supports near-real-time transfers and UPI enables app-based payments using a UPI ID or supported details. Limits, availability and charges depend on the bank and payment system rules.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: neft vs rtgs, imps vs upi, transfer types

---

Q: What is a cheque bounce?
A: A cheque bounce occurs when a bank does not honour a cheque, for example because of insufficient funds, a signature mismatch or another issue. It may lead to bank charges and, in some circumstances, legal consequences. Contact your bank to understand the reason and resolve it promptly.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: cheque bounce, dishonoured cheque, cheque return

---

Q: What is a financial year?
A: In India, the financial year generally runs from 1 April to 31 March of the following year. Income, expenses and tax reporting are commonly organised around this period. The assessment year is the year in which income from the preceding financial year is assessed for tax purposes.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: financial year, fy, assessment year, ay

---

Q: What is a subsidy?
A: A subsidy is financial support provided by a government or authorised body under a specific scheme, subject to eligibility and conditions. Not every loan qualifies for a subsidy, and a subsidy is not the same as a loan waiver. Check the current official scheme guidelines before relying on a benefit.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: subsidy, government benefit, scheme

---

Q: What is a government-backed loan scheme?
A: It is a lending or support programme created or supported by a government body for eligible borrowers or sectors. Each scheme has its own purpose, eligibility, limits, application process and conditions. Availability and benefits must be verified from the official scheme authority or participating lender.
PAGE: general-finance
TAGS: government loan scheme, scheme, supported loan
